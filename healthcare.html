The challenge

A healthcare provider whose patients start the conversation on WhatsApp. Google Ads was spread over dozens of campaigns, many of them spending without producing a single inquiry, and a click or a chat is not yet a patient.

The goal was to turn Google spend into qualified leads (MQL) and booked consultations, not just into clicks, while the client cut the budget partway through.

Reading the charts. Conversions here are WhatsApp clicks and form submissions, as counted in Google Ads. Spend peaked in August, then the client halved the budget in September: conversions held (−2%) on 53% less spend, and in October they doubled at the same spend (+102%).

Beyond clicks: qualified leads

Google conversions overcount real patients, so I judged the work against the CRM. Over the last two 2-week blocks, paid MQLs rose from 16 to 20 (+25%) while Google spend fell 3%, and cost per MQL dropped 24%.

Of the raw leads Google brought in, 36% became MQLs, up to 50% (16 of 44, then 20 of 40).

Further down the funnel, the share of leads that reached a booked consultation doubled from 4.8% to a peak of 9.8% (2-week blocks, all inbound leads), when budget moved to the themes patients actually booked from.

Read with care. The block after the peak was 5.4%, and the latest block is still maturing, so I treat 9.8% as what the funnel can do, not as the steady state. MQL counts are small (single and double digits per week).

How I optimize

  1. Audit campaign by campaignEvery campaign graded on conversions and on the leads that came out of it in the CRM, not on click metrics.
  2. Cut what produced nothingSeven campaigns with zero conversions were spending about 15% of the Google budget. Paused and the money redistributed.
  3. Concentrate on what qualifiesBudget moved to the treatment themes and the hospital-search terms that gave the most MQLs at the lowest cost.
  4. Cross-check against the CRMBecause a WhatsApp click is not a patient, I compare Google conversions with CRM leads every cycle and optimise toward MQL, so volume doesn't hide quality.
